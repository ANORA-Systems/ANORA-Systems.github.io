Part A: General information

Part A applies to both. Personal data is any information relating to an identified or identifiable person, for example a name, an email address or an IP address.

A.3 Legal bases

We process personal data only where one of the following legal bases applies. We state which one applies with each processing activity.

  • Consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). You can withdraw consent at any time with effect for the future.
  • Contract and pre-contractual steps (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR), for example when you use SpecScout or ask for a quote.
  • Legal obligation (Art. 6(1)(c) GDPR), for example retention duties under tax and commercial law.
  • Legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). We name the interest with each processing activity.

Storing information on your device and accessing it, for example through cookies, is also governed by Section 25 of the German Telecommunications Digital Services Data Protection Act (TDDDG). What is stored on your device is described in Part B (website) and Part C (SpecScout).

A.4 Service providers and other recipients

We use service providers. Most of them process data on our behalf (processors under Art. 28 GDPR) and may use it only on our instructions and to provide their service. Some providers also process data under their own responsibility; we point this out with the service concerned (B.1, C.7). We use service providers for the following tasks:

  • servers in Germany for SpecScout and for handling contact and call requests from the website (C.1),
  • email mailboxes and the sending of all emails, including SpecScout's automatic emails (A.8, C.9),
  • error and performance monitoring of our server (A.5),
  • delivering the website (B.1),
  • web search and AI models for searches and AI drafts in SpecScout (C.6).

Which providers these are, with their seat, where they process data and the basis for any transfer to countries outside the EU, is set out in our list of service providers. We keep it up to date. Beyond that, we disclose personal data only where we are legally required to (for example to tax authorities) or where it is necessary to establish, exercise or defend legal claims. We do not sell personal data.

A.5 Server logs and error monitoring

Our server api.anora-systems.com runs SpecScout and receives the contact and call requests from the website. It logs every request with IP address, time, the address called including its parameters (for searches, the search terms) and the result. Some events we log with your user identifier or email address, for example a registration or the sending of an email. We delete the server logs after 30 days at the latest.

For error and performance monitoring we use Sentry (A.4). Sentry receives technical details of requests to this server, that is the address called with its parameters, the browser identification (user agent), response times and error messages, as well as our log messages. These details can include the content of your request, such as search terms, the text of a form or email addresses. We never deliberately send your IP address to Sentry, but in some cases it may be contained in individual reports. Sentry stores the data in the EU and deletes it after at most 90 days. Sentry is based in the USA and is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (A.6).

The legal basis for both is our legitimate interest in detecting errors quickly, investigating abuse and running the service securely and reliably (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

A.6 Transfers to countries outside the EU

Some service providers are based in the USA or other countries outside the EU, or process data there. We transfer personal data to such a third country only where the requirements of Art. 44 et seq. GDPR are met: on the basis of an adequacy decision of the European Commission (Art. 45 GDPR), namely for the United Kingdom and, for the USA, under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework where the recipient is certified under it, and otherwise on the basis of the European Commission's standard contractual clauses (Art. 46(2)(c) GDPR). You can obtain a copy of the standard contractual clauses on request (A.2). The providers concerned and the basis used are set out in the list of service providers.

A.7 Retention

We keep personal data only as long as needed for the purpose concerned. Where we have fixed periods, we state them with each processing activity. Statutory retention duties remain unaffected: depending on their type, we keep business letters and tax-relevant records for six to ten years (Section 257 German Commercial Code, Section 147 German Fiscal Code). During that time we restrict the data and use it only to meet the retention duty.

A.8 Contact by email or phone

If you contact us by email or phone, we process your details (for example name, contact details and the content of your request) to handle your request. The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR if your request relates to a contract or its preparation, otherwise our legitimate interest in answering inquiries (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). We keep emails in our mailboxes at IONOS. We delete your request once it has been fully dealt with, unless a retention duty under A.7 applies.

A.9 Customers on a paid plan

If you or your employer conclude a contract with us for a paid plan, we process the names and contact details of the contact persons as well as contract and billing data in order to conclude, perform and invoice the contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR; for contact persons of a company, Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). We keep invoices and business letters in accordance with A.7.

Part B: Website anora-systems.com

B.1 Hosting on GitHub Pages

The website is delivered through GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc., 88 Colin P. Kelly Jr. Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA. When you open the website, GitHub processes the data technically needed to deliver it, in particular your IP address, the page requested and the time. GitHub logs visitors' IP addresses for security purposes. GitHub processes these logs under its own responsibility and sets their retention period; we have no access to them. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in delivering the website securely and reliably (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). GitHub is certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework (A.6). More in GitHub's privacy statement.

B.2 No cookies, no analytics, no third-party content

The website sets no cookies, stores nothing in your browser and uses no analytics or tracking tools. We serve fonts, icons and scripts ourselves; when you open the website, your browser loads no content from other servers. Your browser connects to our own server api.anora-systems.com only when you send the contact form or a call request (B.3, B.4).

B.3 Contact form

You can send us a message through the contact form. Name, email address and message are required; company, shop system and catalog size are optional. Your browser sends the details to our server api.anora-systems.com at Hetzner in Germany. The server forwards them by email through IONOS to our team's mailboxes, with your email address as the reply address. We do not store the content of your request on the server. Like every request to this server, our server logs record your IP address, the time and the address called, but not the content of your message. Technical details of the request, which can include its content, reach Sentry, our error and performance monitoring service (A.5).

To prevent abuse, the server checks which page the request comes from and processes your IP address: it accepts at most five requests per IP address per hour and only a limited number per hour overall. The counters are held in memory only and expire after one hour. The form also contains a field that is invisible to people. If it is filled in, we assume an automated sender: the message is then not forwarded as a request; instead we receive a warning email with the message and the IP address, so that we can still answer a genuine request that was blocked by mistake. If the overall number for an hour is reached, we receive a notice with the IP address of the first rejected request.

The legal basis for handling your request is Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR if it relates to a contract or its preparation, otherwise our legitimate interest in answering inquiries (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Abuse prevention is based on our legitimate interest in protecting the form and our server from spam and overload (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). We delete the emails as described in A.8, and warning emails once we have checked them.

B.4 Call request

With the call request on the contact page you suggest up to three time slots for a 20-minute call. For this we process your name, your email address and the slots you picked and, if given, your company, phone number and message. If your browser's time zone differs from German time, the request also states the slots in your local time. Technically, the request is handled exactly like a message sent through the contact form (B.3), with the same safeguards, legal bases and deletion periods. Your browser calculates the available slots from our office hours; we do not use a calendar service for this. We confirm one of the slots by email or suggest another one.

B.5 Links to SpecScout

Links such as "Start free" lead to SpecScout at specscout.anora-systems.com. Part C describes what happens to your data there.

Part C: SpecScout

C.1 Overview and hosting

SpecScout consists of the search app at specscout.anora-systems.com, the dashboard at dashboard.anora-systems.com and the SpecScout API at api.anora-systems.com. All three run, together with their database, on our server at Hetzner in Germany (A.4). The search app and the dashboard pass your input to the API through their own server and include your IP address, so that the API can secure sessions, limit abuse and log access.

We process your data in SpecScout to provide the service to you (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR). If your user account belongs to a customer's contract, for example your employer's, that customer is our contracting party; we then process your data on the basis of our legitimate interest in performing the contract with the customer and giving you access to SpecScout for it (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Security measures such as logs and access limits are based on our legitimate interest in operating securely (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Any other legal basis is stated with the processing concerned. A.5 applies to server logs and error monitoring.

C.2 Trial and free access

With "Start free" you enter only your email address on the search app's access page. We use it to create a trial account, with which you can run a few searches right away, and an API key through which the search app searches for you. We send a confirmation link to your address that is valid for 24 hours. Once you confirm it, the account becomes free access with a monthly search quota, and we receive an internal email with your email address and your access level. This lets us detect fraud and abuse of the free access, for example when several accounts are created to circumvent quotas (clause 5(2) of our GTC). The legal basis is our legitimate interest in preventing abuse and in secure operation (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). We delete the email together with your account (C.3). You don't need a password: later you sign in with a link that we send you by email (C.3).

We automatically delete accounts whose email address is not confirmed within 7 days, together with their data; the trial's API key expires at the same time. We use your IP address to create at most five trial accounts per IP address per hour; the counters are held in memory only. The legal basis is providing the service (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) and, for the limit, our legitimate interest in preventing abuse (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

C.3 User account and signing in

In the dashboard you register with an email address and a password; your name is optional. We store the password only as a cryptographic hash. Here, too, you confirm your email address through a link that is valid for 24 hours; after that you have free access, and we receive the same internal email as in C.2. We delete unconfirmed accounts after 7 days, as in C.2. We grant extended access by hand on request and let you know by email. If someone tries to register again with an address that is already registered, its owner receives a notice by email.

You can sign in with your email address and password, with a sign-in link by email that is valid for 15 minutes and works only once, or in the search app with an API key (C.4). We store the links in our emails only as hashes. After five failed password attempts we block signing in with a password for 15 minutes; for this we store the number and time of failed attempts. If you wish, you can protect your account with a second factor (one-time codes from an authenticator app); we store the secret key this requires in encrypted form and the recovery codes only as hashes. Before sensitive actions, such as exporting or deleting your account, you confirm your identity again, with your password or a link by email that is valid for 15 minutes. A link to reset your password is valid for one hour.

For every login we store a session with your IP address, your browser identification and the timestamps. A session ends after at most 30 days, when you log out or when you change your password, and is then deleted.

In the dashboard you can change your name, email address and password, export your data as a file and delete your account yourself; with an account from the search app, you sign in there with a sign-in link. When you delete it, we remove your account together with its sessions, API keys, your own specifications and categories, search settings and quota counters. Entries in the access log (C.4) remain, without a link to your account, until they are deleted after 30 days. Entries in the server logs and at Sentry also remain until they are deleted after 30 days or at most 90 days respectively (A.5). We delete the internal email about your registration (C.2) together with your account. We keep account data as long as the account exists; we delete unconfirmed accounts after 7 days (C.2). User accounts connected to a contract for a paid plan, including custom specifications, categories and search settings, are deleted 30 days after the contract ends, unless statutory retention obligations apply.

C.4 API keys and access log

You create API keys in the dashboard, with a label and an optional expiry date. We show the key only once and store it only as a hash. Expired or revoked keys remain stored until you delete them or your account. If you log in to the search app with an API key, its server keeps the key in memory for the duration of the session.

For every request made with an API key we store an entry in the access log: key and user identifier, the endpoint called (without search parameters), the result, IP address and browser identification. This serves security and the investigation of abuse (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). We delete the entries after 30 days.

C.5 Searches

For a search we process your input: product name, brand, category, requested specifications, options and, where given, domains you prefer or exclude. For every search we store this input together with the values found, the addresses of the source pages and technical measurements, but without a user identifier and without an IP address. After 90 days we condense this data into daily statistics without search content and delete the individual records. For your quota we count searches per account and month, for as long as the account exists. From all searches we also derive figures per website, such as how often a domain yields usable values; they have no link to individual users. The server logs hold the search terms together with the IP address for up to 30 days; for Sentry, see A.5.

To improve SpecScout, we also store a sample for every page an AI model reads for a search: the page's address and text, the model's answer, product name, brand, category and the specification definitions searched for. These samples, too, are stored without a user identifier and without an IP address. We use them to check the quality of the extraction, to compare models and providers and to improve the choice of sources, and we delete them after 24 months. We also use the stored samples to improve our algorithm, in particular to train a ranking algorithm that rates sources and puts them in order. We do not train large language models with them. The page texts can contain personal data of third parties (C.6). The legal basis is Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR; our legitimate interest lies in more accurate extraction, a better choice of sources and thus the data quality of SpecScout.

Searches usually contain only product data. Please do not enter personal data in searches, specifications, categories or descriptions for AI drafts.

C.6 Services used for searches and AI drafts

For searches and AI drafts we pass content of your request to the following kinds of services; which services these are in detail is set out in the list of service providers. These services receive no details about you, that is neither your name nor your email address nor your IP address.

  • Web search: web search services receive search terms built from brand, product, category and up to three specification names, plus your domain filters.
  • Fetching web pages: our server fetches the public pages found itself. The websites visited see our server's IP address, not yours.
  • AI models that read the pages: providers of AI models receive product name, brand, specification definitions and the text of the pages fetched. Which provider answers a request depends on availability and response time; we also keep comparing which models read the pages best. The providers are based in the EU, the United Kingdom or the USA and also process the data in the USA.
  • AI drafts and AI reviews of specifications in the dashboard: your description and existing specification definitions go to providers of AI models.

We base transfers to third countries on an adequacy decision of the European Commission (for the United Kingdom and, for the USA, under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework where the provider is certified) or on the standard contractual clauses (A.6). The web pages fetched can contain personal data of third parties, such as names in reviews. We extract only product information; we store page texts only in the samples described in C.5 and delete them after 24 months. The legal basis is providing the service (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR); where page texts contain personal data of third parties, it is our legitimate interest in analyzing publicly available product information (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

C.7 Product images

If you turn on the option "Include product images" in the search app, your browser loads the images found directly from the websites concerned. These websites then receive your IP address, your browser identification and the address of our app. They process this data under their own responsibility, may set their own cookies and may be based outside the EU. Without this option, the search app loads no images from other websites.

C.8 Your own specifications, categories and search settings

We store the specifications, categories and search settings (preferred and excluded domains) that you create, linked to your account, until you delete them or your account. Our administrators can view them to check them, fix errors and keep the service running (Art. 6(1)(b) and (f) GDPR).

Preferred and excluded domains also feed, without a link to your account, into a shared rating of websites once at least two users have set the same rule. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in improving the choice of sources for all users (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

C.9 Emails from SpecScout

SpecScout only sends you emails about your account or your contract: confirmation and sign-in links, links to confirm sensitive actions and to reset your password, notices that your access level has changed, an invitation when we create an account for you, the notice of a registration attempt with your address, notices of changes to our GTC or to the free access (clause 5(3) GTC), and declarations concerning your contract, such as a termination. They are sent through IONOS (A.4). We do not send advertising.
